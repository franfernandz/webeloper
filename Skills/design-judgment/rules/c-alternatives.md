# Criterion C — Require Exploration of Alternatives

## Rule

Before selecting a direction, the agent must produce three genuinely different concepts — not three variations of the same template. It then compares them and selects one with an explicit rationale.

## Failure Mode It Prevents

The first plausible solution becoming the final design automatically. Without alternatives, the agent has nothing to compare against, so the most immediate idea wins by default.

## What "Genuinely Different" Means

The three concepts must differ in their organizing idea, not in surface details. Changing the accent color, the border radius, or a font size does not create a new concept.

Worked example (three real directions):

- **Direction A — Editorial.** Expressive typography, asymmetric composition, pronounced hierarchy.
- **Direction B — Technical.** Strict grids, precision, annotations, informational density.
- **Direction C — Institutional.** Documentary photography, sobriety, authority.

Each direction implies different type, layout, imagery, and tone. They are not the same site in three palettes.

## Required Comparison

The agent must compare the alternatives on:

1. **Commercial positioning** — which direction best serves the business objective.
2. **Competitive differentiation** — which direction distances the brand from the competitive set.
3. **Implementation viability** — which direction is realistically buildable within scope.

Then it selects one and explains why, referencing the thesis (criterion B).

## Completion Checklist

- Exactly three directions, each describable in one sentence that does not mention color alone.
- A comparison table or equivalent covering the three axes above.
- A stated selection with a rationale that names the runner-up and why it lost.

If the three directions could be swapped between projects without noticing, they are too generic. Rework them.

## Interaction With Other Criteria

- The alternatives must respect the constraint hierarchy (criterion E).
- The discarded decisions from the thesis (criterion B) should reappear here as rejected directions.
- The rationale for the winning direction becomes evidence in the contract (criterion D).

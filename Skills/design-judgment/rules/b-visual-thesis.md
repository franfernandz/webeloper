# Criterion B — Require a Visual Thesis Before Designing

## Rule

Before writing the contract, the agent must define an explicit visual thesis. The thesis is the art direction. Without it, no design work may begin.

## Failure Mode It Prevents

Designing first and rationalizing afterward. When no thesis exists, the agent falls back on statistical patterns and post-hoc justifications like "modern and clean."

## Required Parts of the Thesis

The agent must state all five:

1. **Visual positioning** — what perception the brand must generate in its audience.
2. **Central idea** — the single concept that organizes the whole design.
3. **Aesthetic tension** — the contrast that makes the proposal interesting rather than flat.
4. **Differentiation principle** — what ensures the site is not confused with competitors'.
5. **Deliberately discarded decisions** — which conventional solutions will not be used, and why.

## What Counts as a Thesis

Worked example (fictional industrial consultancy):

> The brand must convey precision and technical authority without looking like a software company. The visual direction draws on technical manuals, documentary industrial photography, and Swiss editorial systems. The tension is between a rigorous grid and large-scale photography. Decorative gradients, abstract illustrations, and SaaS icons are discarded.

That is a direction. The following is not:

> Modern, clean, professional design with a friendly feel.

The second statement could describe any company in any market. It provides no positioning, no tension, and no discarded decisions, so it cannot guide a single choice.

## Completion Checklist

The agent must produce a thesis that is:

- specific to this business and this market;
- impossible to copy verbatim onto a competitor's site;
- explicit about what it rejects;
- writable in a paragraph, not a slogan.

If the thesis could be reused for another client without edits, it is not finished.

## Interaction With Other Criteria

- The thesis supplies the justification required by criteria A and D.
- The discarded decisions in the thesis are the negative space explored by alternatives (criterion C).
- The central idea and tension must be honored by the contract's design system.
